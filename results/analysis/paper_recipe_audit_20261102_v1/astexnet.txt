                                         Featured Application
                                         The proposed AsTexNet framework is suitable for automated texture analysis tasks in
                                         industrial inspection, biomedical imaging, agricultural monitoring, and remote sensing,
                                         where limited labeled data and significant variations in scale and illumination require
                                         robust and compact texture representations.

                                         Abstract
                                         Texture classification remains challenging due to high inter-class similarity, scale variability,
                                         and limited labeled data. While handcrafted descriptors capture fine-grained microstruc-
                                         tures and CNNs encode global semantics, existing hybrid approaches often rely on direct
                                         feature concatenation or heuristic fusion strategies that ignore the varying discriminative
                                         importance of texture scales. Moreover, many deep texture representations introduce high-
                                         dimensional redundant embeddings and require computationally expensive backbone
                                         adaptation, limiting their effectiveness in small-data scenarios. This paper introduces
                                         AsTexNet, a discriminative scale-aware hybrid representation framework that reformulates
                                         multi-scale feature integration as a structured weighting problem. The proposed method
                                         quantifies the class-separability contribution of each handcrafted scale using a data-driven
                                         weighting mechanism, enabling adaptive emphasis on informative texture patterns. In
                                         parallel, pretrained CNN features are enhanced using Generalized Mean (GeM) pooling
                                         and compacted via cross-validated PCA to improve separability while controlling redun-
                                         dancy. A normalized late-fusion formulation integrates local and global cues into a compact
                                         512-dimensional embedding without requiring backbone fine-tuning. Extensive experi-
                                         ments on six benchmark datasets demonstrate consistent performance gains, achieving
                                         100% accuracy on Kylberg and Brodatz, 99.4% on UIUC, 99.0% on UMD, 90.6% on KTH-
                                         TIPS2b, and 78.71% on FMD. These results demonstrate that explicitly modeling scale-wise
                                         discriminative contributions leads to more effective and compact texture representations,

                            Despite the success of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in generic image classification,
                            texture analysis remains particularly challenging. The primary difficulties arise from high
                            inter-class similarity, subtle microstructural variations, the absence of strong global shape
                            cues, and the limited availability of annotated datasets—especially in medical and scientific
                            imaging applications [5,6]. Training CNNs from scratch in such small-data settings often
                            leads to overfitting, while fine-tuning large-scale pretrained models (e.g., ImageNet-based
                            networks) may suffer from domain mismatch, in which semantic object-level features are
                            not optimally aligned with texture-centric representations.
                                  To mitigate these issues, hybrid approaches have been introduced that combine CNN-
                            based representations with handcrafted texture descriptors [7–9]. In parallel, transfer
                            learning has become a widely adopted strategy, where pretrained CNNs are used as fixed
                            feature extractors [10–12]. These approaches typically leverage mid-level convolutional
                            activations that encode structural patterns rather than object-level semantics [13]. However,
                            several limitations remain. First, many hybrid discriminative integration strategies rely
                            on naive concatenation or heuristic weighting schemes that do not explicitly quantify the
                            discriminative contribution of different texture scales. Second, deep backbones employed
                            in transfer-learning pipelines are often computationally heavy and optimized for large-scale
                            object datasets rather than texture-dominant tasks. Third, feature integration mechanisms
                            frequently overlook redundancy and dimensional inefficiency, leading to high-dimensional
                            representations without proportional gains in separability. Even classical encoding methods
                            such as Fisher Vectors [14], while effective, often result in very large descriptors and
                            increased computational burden. Recent advances in deep visual recognition and texture
                            analysis have demonstrated the effectiveness of discriminative feature representations for
                            texture and material recognition [15–18].
                                  Therefore, despite extensive progress, a fundamental challenge remains unresolved:
                            how to construct a compact, computationally efficient, and discriminatively structured
                            hybrid representation that systematically integrates multi-scale handcrafted texture cues
                            with pretrained deep features while explicitly controlling dimensional growth and ensuring
                            stability under limited-data constraints.
                                  Motivated by this gap, we introduce AsTexNet (Adaptive Scale Texture Network),
                            a structured hybrid framework designed around three core principles: discriminative
                            scale quantification, compact embedding construction, and stable heterogeneous feature
                            discriminative integration. Rather than relying on brute-force deep fine-tuning or naive
                            high-dimensional concatenation, AsTexNet explicitly measures the class-separability con-
                            tribution of multi-scale handcrafted descriptors and reweights them through a data-driven
                            mechanism prior to integration. These reweighted descriptors are fused with compact CNN
                            representations enhanced via Generalized Mean (GeM) pooling [19] and cross-validated
                            PCA compression [20], thereby improving linear separability while constraining redun-
                            dancy. The proposed design balances global semantic abstraction with fine-grained lo-
                            cal statistics, achieving an explicit accuracy–compactness trade-off suited for small- and
                            medium-scale texture datasets.
                                  The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
                            1.   A novel discriminative scale-aware weighting algorithm that reformulates multi-scale
                                 handcrafted feature integration as a supervised scale-selection problem. Each scale-
                                 specific descriptor block is evaluated using multinomial logistic regression, and the
                                 normalized L2 norm of the learned coefficients is used to quantify its discriminative
                                 importance. These weights adaptively emphasize informative texture scales before
                                 hybrid fusion, providing a principled alternative to the heuristic or uniform fusion
                                 strategies commonly used in existing hybrid texture classification methods.

                            2. Related Work
                                   Early research in texture recognition primarily relied on handcrafted descriptors de-
                            signed to achieve invariance to scale, illumination, and rotation. Representative methods
                            include Scale-Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT) [21], local binary patterns (LBPs) [22],
                            and their numerous extensions [23,24]. These approaches typically required an additional
                            encoding stage to aggregate local descriptors into global representations suitable for clas-
                            sification. Classical aggregation strategies such as Bag-of-Visual-Words (BoVW) [25–28],
                            Vector of Locally Aggregated Descriptors (VLAD) [29], and Fisher Vectors (FVs) [30,31]
                            improved discriminative power but often resulted in high-dimensional representations
                            and increased computational complexity. Moreover, handcrafted pipelines were limited in
                            capturing higher-order semantic patterns beyond local statistics.
                                   With the rise of deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) became domi-
                            nant in texture analysis. Due to the relatively small size of most texture datasets, CNNs
                            were frequently used as fixed feature extractors rather than being trained from scratch.
                            For example, Cimpoi et al. [14] combined VGG [32] with Fisher Vector encoding, demon-
                            strating that deep filter banks substantially outperform classical handcrafted descriptors.
                            Song et al. [33] enhanced local distinctiveness through locally connected layers, while
                            Jbene et al. [34] proposed a two-stream hybrid architecture that fused handcrafted SIFT
                            features with CNN representations via fully connected layers. Their method, tested on
                            Xception [35] and ResNet-50 [36], achieved further improvements in classification accuracy.
                            Although these methods improved accuracy, they often relied on high-dimensional feature
                            concatenation or heavy backbone architectures, leading to increased computational costs
                            and limited efficiency under small-data regimes.
                                   Subsequent research focused on end-to-end CNN-based encoding mechanisms. Zhang
                            et al. [37] introduced an orderless texture encoding layer based on learned dictionary assign-
                            ment, while Xue et al. [38] incorporated bilinear pooling to model higher-order statistics.



                                                                                         https://doi.org/10.3390/app16125743
Appl. Sci. 2026, 16, 5743                                                                                            4 of 30



                            Chen et al. [10] leveraged multi-layer feature aggregation combined with fractal descriptors,
                            and Mao et al. [11] proposed residual pooling strategies that avoided explicit dictionary
                            learning. Yang et al. [12] further integrated frequency-domain attention mechanisms, and
                            Xu et al. [39] developed fractal average pooling for improved discriminative modeling.
                            Recent work has also explored multimodal and frequency-domain feature integration strate-
                            gies to improve representation robustness. For example, spatial–frequency–optical flow
                            fusion has been investigated for AIGC video detection, demonstrating the effectiveness of
                            multimodal feature integration for discriminative analysis [40]. Similarly, Fourier-based
                            feature fusion approaches have shown strong capability in challenging visual restoration
                            tasks such as marine snow removal [41]. In parallel, texture classification research has
                            increasingly focused on hybrid representation learning frameworks that integrate hand-
                            crafted texture descriptors with deep semantic features to exploit their complementary
                            strengths. Such approaches combine local texture cues, multi-scale structural informa-
                            tion, and deep feature representations to enhance discriminative capability, robustness
                            to intra-class variability, and generalization across challenging texture datasets [42–45].
                            Furthermore, texture-aware deep learning models and feature fusion architectures have
                            demonstrated that complementary representations can improve classification accuracy
                            while mitigating the limitations of individual handcrafted or deep descriptors. These ad-
                            vances underscore the growing importance of structured feature integration and compact
                            hybrid representations for robust texture recognition.
                                 While these approaches enhanced representational power, they generally required
                            deeper architectures, additional learnable modules, or complex bilinear pooling mecha-
                            nisms, making them computationally demanding and sensitive to dataset scale.
                                 Another research direction sought to avoid fine-tuning while exploiting powerful
                            pretrained backbones. Scabini et al. [46] employed randomized autoencoders on ConvNeXt
                            features [47], and Florindo et al. [48] transformed CNN activations into graph-based
                            descriptors. Other works explored entropy-based transformations [49] and fuzzy encoding
                            schemes [50]. Although these methods reduced the need for end-to-end training, they often
                            produced high-dimensional descriptors or introduced additional transformation stages
                            without explicitly analyzing the discriminative contribution of individual texture scales.
                                 Despite significant progress, several fundamental limitations persist in existing hybrid
                            texture recognition frameworks. First, many fusion strategies rely on heuristic concatena-
                            tion or uniform weighting, without explicitly quantifying the discriminative contribution
                            of individual multi-scale handcrafted components. As a result, scale selection remains
                            empirical rather than data-driven. Second, deep feature aggregation methods often increase
                            descriptor dimensionality without formally controlling redundancy or improving linear
                            separability, leading to inefficient high-dimensional embeddings. Third, most integration
                            strategies prioritize performance maximization while overlooking representation compact-
                            ness, numerical stability, and robustness under limited-data regimes—conditions that are
                            typical in texture recognition tasks.
                                 Motivated by these unresolved challenges, the proposed AsTexNet framework intro-
                            duces a structured hybrid representation design centered on three principles: discriminative
                            scale quantification, compact embedding construction, and stable heterogeneous feature
                            integration. Unlike naive concatenation-based hybrids, AsTexNet employs a scale-wise
                            discriminative weighting mechanism that explicitly measures class-separability contribu-
                            tions of each handcrafted scale via multinomial logistic regression, enabling data-driven
                            global reweighting without introducing additional learnable fusion parameters. To mitigate
                            redundancy in deep representations, the framework enhances pretrained CNN features
                            using Generalized Mean pooling followed by cross-validated PCA compression, improv-
                            ing linear separability while constraining dimensional growth. Furthermore, weighted



                                                                                        https://doi.org/10.3390/app16125743
Appl. Sci. 2026, 16, 5743                                                                                             5 of 30



                            structured integration is performed within a normalized feature space to ensure stable
                            integration of heterogeneous cues and prevent dominance of high-variance components.
                            Although the proposed framework ultimately produces a compact 512-dimensional em-
                            bedding, its hybrid architecture introduces additional computational overhead due to
                            multi-scale handcrafted feature extraction, discriminative scale-weight estimation, and
                            PCA-based projection of deep representations. In addition, the framework relies on pre-
                            trained CNN representations and multiple processing stages, which may limit its suitability
                            for real-time or resource-constrained deployment scenarios. Consequently, AsTexNet is
                            computationally more demanding than conventional handcrafted descriptors such as LBP
                            (54-D, 0.058–0.069 s/image) and CLBP (3552-D, 0.095–0.108 s/image), while remaining sub-
                            stantially more efficient than high-dimensional deep feature extraction frameworks such as
                            AlexNet (32,768-D, 0.336–0.404 s/image) and VGG-VD (65,536-D, 0.410–0.492 s/image).
                            Despite requiring approximately 0.29 s per image on average, the proposed framework
                            achieves a favorable trade-off between discriminative performance, feature compactness,
                            and computational efficiency, thereby making it suitable for robust texture classification
                            under limited-data conditions.
                                 Building upon these principles, the detailed architecture of the proposed framework
                            is presented in the following section.

                            3. Proposed AsTexNet Framework
                                  The proposed AsTexNet framework integrates global convolutional representations
                            with multi-scale handcrafted texture descriptors through a structured hybrid design. The
                            objective is to combine high-level semantic features extracted by deep networks with stable
                            microstructural statistics derived from handcrafted encodings, thereby improving represen-
                            tational complementarity, particularly in limited-data scenarios. The overall architecture is
                            illustrated in Figure 1.
                                  Let the dataset be a fixed, pre-split set D = Dtrain ∪ Dtest with C classes. Each image
                            I ∈ R H ×W ×3 is resized to S × S (here S = 256). For each image, two complementary repre-
                            sentations are extracted: a global deep descriptor and a multi-scale handcrafted descriptor.
                            These are reweighted, fused, and mapped to a compact embedding for classification.
                                  Symbols: ||.||2 (Euclidean norm), 1[.] (Indicator function), ⊙ (Hadamard), ⊕ (con-
                            catenation operator).
                                  The framework consists of two complementary branches. The global branch employs
                            a MobileNetV2 [51] backbone pretrained on ImageNet to extract semantic feature maps. A
                            two-crop strategy (original and horizontally flipped inputs) is applied to increase invari-
                            ance, and Generalized Mean (GeM) pooling aggregates spatial activations into compact
                            1280-dimensional descriptors. These descriptors are L2-normalized and projected using
                            Principal Component Analysis (PCA), where the projection dimensionality is selected
                            through cross-validation.
                                  The local branch computes multi-scale handcrafted descriptors. For each radius–
                            neighborhood configuration, three complementary encodings are derived: intensity pattern
                            histograms, gradient-magnitude histograms (99th percentile clipped), and center-intensity
                            codes (75th percentile thresholded). These descriptors characterize local structural patterns,
                            edge magnitude distribution, and global contrast statistics. A scale-wise discriminative
                            weighting mechanism based on multinomial logistic regression estimates the relative
                            contribution of each scale. The normalized histograms are concatenated and reweighted
                            prior to fusion.

                                 In this context, AsTexNet shares certain characteristics with prior approaches that
                            employ pretrained backbones without fine-tuning. However, it adopts a fundamentally
                            different representation strategy tailored specifically for texture analysis. Rather than rely-
                            ing exclusively on global CNN activations or on handcrafted descriptors in isolation, the
                            proposed framework integrates compact semantic embeddings derived from MobileNetV2
                            with discriminatively reweighted multi-scale handcrafted texture statistics through a struc-
                            tured late-fusion formulation.
                                 The adaptive scale reweighting mechanism enables data-driven emphasis on the most
                            informative spatial configurations, preserving fine-grained texture details while attenuating
                            less discriminative scales. Furthermore, the combination of PCA-based dimensionality re-
                            duction and cosine-normalized classification enhances embedding compactness, numerical
                            stability, and inter-class separability.
                                 Overall, the proposed architecture achieves consistent and competitive performance
                            while maintaining computational efficiency, as it avoids additional backbone fine-tuning
                            and introduces only lightweight fusion and classification components. This design ensures
                            a controlled balance between representational richness and efficiency, which is particularly
                            advantageous in limited-data texture recognition scenarios.
                                 Since discriminative scale weights are estimated once on the training set and reused
                            during inference, the additional computational overhead of the proposed weighting mech-
                            anism is negligible relative to feature extraction and classification.
                                 To validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework in terms of classification
                            accuracy, representation compactness, and computational efficiency, extensive experiments
                            were conducted on six benchmark texture datasets.

                            4.2. Evaluation Protocol and Implementation Details
                                 All experiments strictly follow the original predefined train–test splits provided by
                            each dataset. Classification accuracy (%) is reported on the test split. All hyperparameters,
                            including PCA dimensionality, scale weights, and fusion coefficients, were determined
                            exclusively using the training split through cross-validation. The test set was accessed only
                            once for final performance evaluation. No preprocessing, normalization, PCA fitting, or
                            scale-weight estimation was performed jointly on training and test samples, ensuring strict
                            separation between splits and preventing data leakage.
                                 Data augmentation was intentionally limited to horizontal flipping in order to pre-
                            serve fine-grained texture statistics and maintain consistency between handcrafted and
                            CNN representations. No random cropping, random rotation, color jittering, or geometric
                            deformation was applied, as such transformations may distort local texture distributions
                            and affect handcrafted descriptor stability. Hyperparameters including PCA projection
                            dimensionality and fusion coefficients (α, β) were selected using 5-fold cross-validation per-
                            formed exclusively on the training split. For each fold, PCA fitting, feature standardization,
                            and discriminative scale-weight estimation were recomputed independently using only the
                            corresponding training partition to avoid information leakage. For reproducibility, all exper-
                            iments used a fixed random seed of 42 across NumPy, PyTorch, and scikit-learn operations,
                            with deterministic execution settings enabled whenever supported by the framework.
                                 All experiments were implemented in Python 3.11 using PyTorch 2.2, torchvision 0.17,
                            scikit-learn 1.4, and NumPy 1.26 on macOS with Apple Metal Performance Shaders (MPS)
                            acceleration. The MobileNetV2 backbone was used in pretrained form without fine-tuning.
                            Optimization of the cosine-MLP classifier employed the Adam optimizer with an initial
                            learning rate of 1 × 10−3 , batch size of 32, and weight decay of 1 × 10−4 . Training used




                                                                                           https://doi.org/10.3390/app16125743
Appl. Sci. 2026, 16, 5743                                                                                           15 of 30



                            a Reduce-on-Plateau learning rate schedule, early stopping based on validation accuracy,
                            and gradient clipping for stability.
                                  Feature extraction outputs (CNN descriptors, handcrafted histograms, PCA projec-
                            tions, and fusion parameters) were cached to avoid recomputation and guarantee determin-
                            istic evaluation. Mixed-precision inference and efficient tensor formatting were adopted to
                            accelerate computation without affecting numerical results.

                            4.3.2. Dimensionality and Computational Complexity
                                  The comparison of feature dimensionality and computation time for different tex-
                            ture descriptors (Table 5) shows a clear trade-off between representation compactness
                            and computational cost. Traditional handcrafted methods such as Original LBP (54-D)
                            and CJLBPABMV (400-D) have the shortest computational times, averaging 0.069 s on
                            KTH-TIPS2b and 0.058 s on Brodatz, which is due to their straightforward binary pat-
                            tern encoding. Techniques like CLBP (3552-D) and BRINT (1296-D) closely follow the
                            microstructures and thus they have longer processing times, which are usually between
                            0.095 s and 0.178 s per image.
                                  In contrast, feature representations based on deep CNNs have considerably higher
                            dimensionality. To illustrate, AlexNet (32,768-D) and VGG-VD (65,536-D) are the ones that
                            cause the largest computational cost (roughly 0.336–0.492 s per image) due to their deeper
                            architectures and global activation patterns.
                                  Hybrid models integrating handcrafted and neural cues, such as LGONBP (1404-D)
                            and 6LBP + CellNN (192-D), maintain moderate feature dimensions and balanced com-
                            putation, achieving times between 0.085 and 0.212 s. The proposed AsTexNet (512-D)
                            achieves a strong balance between discriminative richness and efficiency, requiring only
                            0.29 s per image on both datasets—significantly lower than high-dimensional deep
                            models—demonstrating its compact yet powerful feature integration with discrimina-
                            tive weighting design.
                                  The results indicate that AsTexNet provides a balance between feature dimensionality
                            and computational cost. With a 512-dimensional representation, the proposed framework
                            achieves accuracy comparable to several higher-dimensional deep models while maintain-
                            ing moderate processing time per image. These findings further highlight the compact and
                            efficient nature of the proposed framework.

                            6. Conclusions
                                  This paper presented AsTexNet, which reformulates hybrid texture representation
                            as a discriminative scale-aware feature integration problem by combining pretrained Mo-
                            bileNetV2 features with multi-scale handcrafted descriptors through structured weighting
                            and a compact embedding design. The proposed design emphasizes compact representa-
                            tion learning and modular integration rather than end-to-end backbone retraining.
                                  Comprehensive evaluation across six benchmark datasets demonstrates that the pro-
                            posed framework achieves consistently strong performance relative to representative hand-
                            crafted, deep, and hybrid approaches. In addition to accuracy, the method maintains a
                            compact 512-dimensional representation and moderate computational cost, highlighting its
                            suitability for limited-data regimes and resource-constrained scenarios. The ablation analy-
                            sis further confirms that multi-scale handcrafted encoding, GeM pooling, PCA compression,
                            and hybrid classification each contribute incrementally to the final performance.
                                  The proposed AsTexNet framework shows potential for practical texture recogni-
                            tion applications, including industrial surface inspection, medical image analysis, ma-
                            terial classification, remote sensing, and agricultural monitoring. Owing to its compact
                            embedding formulation and lightweight architecture, the framework is particularly suit-
                            able for deployment in resource-constrained environments requiring efficient and reliable
                            texture classification.
                                  Despite these strengths, several limitations remain. First, the framework relies on fixed
                            pretrained CNN features and does not support fully end-to-end optimization, which may
                            restrict task-specific adaptation. Second, the discriminative scale weighting mechanism is
                            computed offline and remains non-learnable, potentially limiting adaptability to domain
                            shifts. Third, although the method performs robustly across evaluated datasets, extreme
                            variations such as severe occlusion, strong illumination distortion, or large cross-domain
                            discrepancies may reduce generalization capability.
                                  Future work will focus on extending the framework toward a fully end-to-end train-
                            able architecture incorporating attention-guided or learnable discriminative integration
                            mechanisms. Exploring transformer-based encoders for improved global context mod-
                            eling represents another promising direction. Additionally, adapting the framework for
                            multimodal and hyperspectral texture analysis, optimizing inference for real-time em-
                            bedded deployment, and conducting large-scale cross-domain evaluations could further
                            strengthen its applicability in industrial inspection, agricultural monitoring, and biomedical
                            imaging tasks.
