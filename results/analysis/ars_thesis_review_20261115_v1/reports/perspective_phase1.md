## Contract Paraphrase

D1 — Methodology rigor requires a study design, treatment of data, statistical account, and reproducibility information that satisfy the field's review expectations. From an adjacent reader's perspective, those elements must permit a clear understanding of how the evidence was obtained, while assessment of their technical adequacy belongs to the methodology reviewer.

D2 — Domain accuracy requires correct technical concepts and results, faithful treatment of prior research, and claims consistent with established evidence in the relevant field. Cross-disciplinary interpretation must preserve those meanings; the domain reviewer owns the judgment of technical accuracy.

D3 — Argumentative coherence requires the central proposition to remain consistent throughout the work and its conclusions to follow from the evidence without reasoning errors that compromise the argument. An adjacent reader should be able to trace that connection, while the designated argument reviewers assess this dimension.

D4 — Cross-disciplinary relevance requires framing, definitions, and implications that a reader from a neighboring discipline can understand, together with adequate support for any claims extending across disciplines. This high-priority dimension is the perspective reviewer's responsibility.

D5 — Writing and structure require an intelligible organization, clear explanations, usable figures and tables, and observance of the applicable publication conventions. These features help neighboring-field readers navigate the work, but the editor owns their formal assessment.

D6 — Venue fit and contribution require an original and meaningful contribution suited to the configured outlet and its audience. An adjacent-field perspective can help identify how the contribution is communicated, but the editor owns this mandatory dimension and no target-specific alignment can be presumed without supplied criteria.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Assess whether the problem and purpose are understandable to adjacent-field readers; whether central terms, notation, procedures, and comparison conditions receive sufficient explanation; whether implications distinguish the demonstrated setting from broader uses; and whether any interdisciplinary claims have explicit supporting evidence.
what_triggers_block: Essential framing or definitions are missing or ambiguous enough that an adjacent-field reader cannot reliably understand the central contribution or its implications, or a central interdisciplinary claim depends on unsupported transfer beyond the demonstrated setting and requires substantive revision.
what_triggers_warn: The central contribution remains understandable, but localized unexplained terminology, incomplete contextualization, or loosely qualified implications create remediable obstacles for adjacent-field readers without undermining their overall interpretation.
