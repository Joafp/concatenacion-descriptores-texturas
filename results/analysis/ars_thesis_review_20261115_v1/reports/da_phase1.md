## Contract Paraphrase

D1 — Methodological rigor requires an empirical design, treatment of data, uncertainty reporting, and reproducibility provisions that withstand serious challenges to the validity of the findings.

D2 — Domain accuracy requires technically correct statements and faithful treatment of relevant evidence and earlier scholarship; an adversarial reading must distinguish established knowledge from unsupported assertions.

D3 — Argumentative coherence requires the central proposition, supporting evidence, and conclusions to fit together without contradiction or an inferential gap that defeats the thesis.

D4 — Cross-disciplinary relevance requires readers from neighboring fields to understand the framing and concepts, while claims of wider relevance must have adequate support.

D5 — Writing and structure require an intelligible progression, clear exposition, useful figures and tables, and appropriate presentation conventions so that the reasoning can be assessed.

D6 — Fit and contribution require a defensible account of originality, importance, and relevance to the intended readership; any assessment against venue requirements depends on an actually supplied target.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Trace each central claim from stated premises through reported evidence to conclusions; challenge consistency, scope, alternative explanations, and the distinction between observation and inference.
what_triggers_block: A central conclusion requires an unsupported inferential step, contradicts another central assertion, or exceeds what the reported evidence can establish, requiring substantial reconstruction of the argument.
what_triggers_warn: A localized ambiguity, overstatement, or incompletely addressed alternative weakens a subsidiary inference but can be corrected without changing the central proposition or evidential basis.
what_triggers_fatal: The central proposition is logically incompatible with its own defining premises or indispensable reported evidence, such that repairing the contradiction requires abandoning the thesis's central claim.
