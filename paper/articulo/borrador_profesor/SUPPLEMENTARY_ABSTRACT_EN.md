# Supplementary Abstract (English) — For journal submission

**Title:** Block-level selection and concatenation of heterogeneous descriptors for texture classification

**Abstract:** This work quantifies how much of the gain from concatenating texture descriptors stems from complementarity versus gathering individually strong representations. Twenty frozen descriptors (5 classical: LBP/DRLBP/Gabor/GLCM/HOG + 15 foundation: VGG/ResNet/DenseNet/EfficientNet/ConvNeXt/ViT/DeiT/Swin/EVA-02/DINOv2/MAE/SigLIP) were compared on DTD, FMD, CUReT and Outex with linear SVM and ResMLP under a strict nested protocol (4 inner folds, outer test never touched). Greedy Forward Selection (GFS, k≤8, 2× improvement <0.001 stopping, best historic) outperformed the best individual chosen by inner validation in all six DTD/FMD/CUReT means and both Outex evaluations, but four differences lay within the pre-registered practical margin |Δ|<0.01; dimensionality dropped 69.8–85.2%. However, GFS showed no aggregate advantage over a top-$k$ control with equal budget (26 wins, 26 losses, 4 ties over 56 outer conditions), indicating that gathering a few strong blocks explains most of the gain. On the official 680/680 Outex split, the reproduced RGB Pixel N-grams (0.9632) concatenated with 20 blocks reached 0.9691 (Δ=4 hits, paired bootstrap 95% CI [-0.0088,0.0206], McNemar p=0.585, n.s.). Group Lasso (40 fits vs ≤640, 8–16× cheaper) reproduced the tie within ±0.01 with slightly higher Jaccard (0.48 vs 0.43). GPU wall-clock on RTX 4060 (batch=1): DINOv2-L 167.5ms, DINOv2 52.7ms, ViT-B 8.5ms, ResNet50 8.8ms, LBP 0.48ms (CPU); full concatenation 393.9ms vs GFS 267.7ms vs top-$k$ 235ms — concatenation is Pareto-dominated. Results show concatenation can raise the ceiling but selection compresses; neither dominates in accuracy, dimensionality, cost and stability.

**Keywords:** texture classification, descriptor concatenation, block feature selection, Greedy Forward Selection, representation fusion
